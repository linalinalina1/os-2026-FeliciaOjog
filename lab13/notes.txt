Lab 13 - Semaphores in Your Own Kernel (xv6)
Observe questions and answers

1. Without the lock, why do the letters mix? How many system calls does one line cost?

The mix program sends every character through its own write(1, p, 1) system call. Its two processes may be scheduled between these calls, so A and b characters from different processes appear on the same line. Each generated line has 30 letters followed by a newline, requiring 31 write() system calls. Both unlocked runs showed actual mixed output, with different interleavings.

2. With the lock, every line is whole. Which two lines of mix.c make one line a critical section?

sem_down(3) before the loop of write() calls and sem_up(3) after the loop. In the observed mix lock execution, the output contained five complete A lines followed by five complete b lines. The specific order may change, but characters cannot intermingle within the protected line.

3. In sem_down the test is while (s->value == 0), not if. Why can a process wake up and still find 0?

A wakeup only allows a waiting process to try again. Another runnable process may claim a permit first, or several processes can awaken for fewer available permits. Rechecking with while prevents a process from continuing when the count is still zero.

4. sem_down calls release(&s->lock) BEFORE sleep(). What would go wrong if it slept holding the spinlock?

The sleeping process would prevent another process from acquiring the spinlock in sem_up(), so the count could not be incremented and waiters could not be awakened. That would block progress and could cause deadlock. The implementation calls sleep_prepare() while protected, then releases the spinlock before sleep(), as required by this xv6 variant.
